    perf(trimul): the bidirectional back half fuses, and 18 ladders come down to what wins
    
    Two things the A6000 module bench made visible.
    
    1. `triangle_multiplication_bidirectional` lost to cuequivariance -- 19.32 ms against 18.12 at
       L=1024 -- while the UNIdirectional module beat it. Not the cache: the bidirectional target hit
       the cache on every op but one. The difference is which kernels it reaches. Unidirectional runs
       `trimul_outproj_layernorm_gemm_gate_triton`, which does LN + proj GEMM + gate GEMM + residual in
       one pass; bidirectional ran `_te_forward` then `gate_elem_infer`, two passes with a materialised
       (M, N) proj between them.
    
       It was refused, not overlooked: `_back_kernel` normalised `tri` over K and gated `x_n` over the
       SAME K, and the bidirectional shape is tri 2*d_hidden wide against x_n d_pair. The launcher said
       so -- "d_hidden != d_pair needs the split back half".
    
       So the gate gets its own contraction width. `KG` is x_n's width and Wg's first axis, with its
       own accumulation loop; at KG == K every loop is single-trip and the square path is what it was.
       KG is a constexpr the kernel tiles over, so it is in the shape key.
    
       Measured on an A6000 at L=1024, d_pair=128, back half alone: 5.05 ms (2.79 + 2.27) -> 2.80 ms,
       1.81x. Against a fp32 reference the fused path is also the more accurate of the two (rel 1.84e-3
       vs 2.02e-3): one fewer bf16 round trip through the proj it no longer materialises.
    
       Module, after rebuilding the 12 units this invalidates -- benching before that would have
       measured the heuristic fallback:
    
         bidirectional inference  19.32 -> 17.36 ms   (cuequiv 18.26)   0.92x -> 1.05x
         bidirectional training   145.1 -> 67.86 ms   (cuequiv 72.38)            1.07x
         unidirectional inference  8.59 ->  8.89 ms   (cuequiv  9.23)            1.04x
    
    2. `test_a_ladder_offers_what_wins` went red when the gap-rebuild caches landed, which is the test
       working: enough kernels crossed "cache covers a whole planned build" for it to start asking that
       their ladders match what actually wins. 18 edits over 13 kernels, 17 of them narrowings.
    
       A narrowing costs nothing to adopt -- `cache_status` treats a grid change as incremental, not
       STALE, and `store_ranked_configs` drops the configs that left -- and the sweep goes from
       1,822,605 to 1,693,881 (config, shape), 122 to 113 GPU-hours. The one widening,
       `adaln_fwd_gate_triton` num_warps + 1, owes a top-up build.
